Columns~(1)--(3) restrict to age 65 and older (this paper's main sample, comparable to column~(5) of Table~\ref{t:combined_elderly}); columns~(4)--(9) restrict to age 51 and older, matching Gertler's (2000) age cutoff, under two alternative age definitions. Unlike the difference-in-differences specifications elsewhere in this paper, which follow the 1997 baseline cohort over time, this table is a pooled cross-section and is therefore constructed directly from the June and November 1999 household rosters. It accordingly includes individuals who entered sample households after the 1997 baseline, as Gertler's cross-section does. Columns~(4)--(6) measure age contemporaneously at each wave's own interview and restrict each wave to the individuals it actually enumerated. Columns~(7)--(9) instead measure age at the 1997 baseline interview (\textit{age97}); because baseline age is observed only for individuals present in 1997, these columns implicitly re-impose the baseline-cohort restriction, and their smaller sample relative to columns~(4)--(6) measures the post-1997 entrant population. They should be read as a diagnostic on the age definition rather than as an alternative specification. Compare column~(4)'s (or column~(7)'s) observation count to Gertler's reported $N=15{,}399$: pooling the two waves is expected to roughly double the single-wave sample used in Table~\ref{t:combined_elderly}. Standard errors are clustered at the locality level and shown in parentheses. ***p$<0.01$, **p$<0.05$, *p$<0.1$.}

    \caption{Event Study: $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$ Coefficient by Numerator and Denominator Construction}
    \label{af:intensity_construction_comparison}

{\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/appendix/Figure_2_all.pdf}}

         \floatfoot{Notes: This figure overlays event-study estimates of the interaction between $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$ and year indicators (reference year 1996) for the pooled older-adult (65+) mortality rate per 1,000 inhabitants, weighted and with the Seguro Popular control, under the four constructions of Table~\ref{t:did_age_fixeddenom}, crossing the beneficiary-numerator choice (Mixed vs.\ FASE) with the household-denominator choice (year-varying vs.\ fixed P\&V-style), so the movement of the coefficient across constructions is directly visible on one set of axes. ``Mixed, year-varying'' is the current default: a point-in-time headcount (FASE for 1997, the newer admin pipeline taken directly from 1998 on) divided by each year's own municipal household count; it need not be monotonic. ``FASE, year-varying'' replaces the numerator with Parker and Vogl's (2023) own construction, a genuine running sum of annual FASE beneficiary counts, keeping the year-varying denominator. ``Mixed, fixed P\&V'' keeps the current numerator but divides by a single, time-invariant household count fixed at an interpolated 1997 base. ``FASE, fixed P\&V'' combines both changes, matching Parker and Vogl (2023) exactly. Unlike Table~\ref{t:did_age_fixeddenom}, $\textit{Intensity}_{2005}$ is omitted from these specifications since the comparison here concerns the construction of $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$ itself. The four series are offset slightly on the x-axis so points and confidence intervals do not overlap. The vertical line marks the program's 1997 rollout. The sample is restricted to highly marginalized municipalities. 95\% confidence intervals shown.}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \caption{Event Study: Binary High-vs-Low $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$, Threshold Sensitivity}
    \label{af:binary_es}

{\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/appendix/AF_binary_es.pdf}}

         \floatfoot{Notes: This figure presents event-study estimates from a binarized version of the main specification: municipalities are classified as ``high'' or ``low'' $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$ using three alternative thresholds (15 percent; the highly-marginalized-sample median; the 75th percentile), and the interaction between this binary indicator and year fixed effects (reference year 1996) is estimated for the older-adult (65+) mortality rate per 1,000 inhabitants. All specifications include Seguro Popular intensity and municipality fixed effects, and are weighted by the population aged 65 and older. The vertical line marks the program's 1997 rollout. The sample is restricted to highly marginalized municipalities. Because program penetration was near-universal within this sample by 2000, there is no meaningful never-treated group at any threshold; ``low'' should be read as below-threshold $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$, not as an untreated comparison group. This binarization tests robustness to the dose-response functional form (i.e., linearity in intensity) and does not itself address the forbidden-comparisons concern, which the single 1997 break already rules out irrespective of whether treatment is measured continuously or as a binary indicator. Appendix Table~\ref{at:binary_es} reports the corresponding standard difference-in-differences coefficient and the number of high- and low-intensity municipalities under each threshold. 95\% confidence intervals shown.}

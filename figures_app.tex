\begin{figure}[H]
    \caption{Event Study: Stability of the Early-Phase (Intensity 1999) Coefficient to the Choice of Second-Phase Control}
    \label{af:beta0_stability}

{\includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/appendix/AF8_beta0_stability.pdf}}

         \floatfoot{Notes: This figure presents event-study estimates of the interaction between $\textit{Intensity}_{1999}$ and year indicators (reference year 1996) for the older-adult (65+) mortality rate per 1,000 inhabitants, under five specifications. The first, ``No control,'' omits any second-phase intensity control entirely, illustrating the misspecification that arises when later cumulative enrollment is left uncontrolled for. The remaining three add a second-phase (later cumulative enrollment) control: the baseline specification controlling for $\textit{Intensity}_{2005}$, and specifications replacing it with the adjacent $\textit{Intensity}_{2004}$ and $\textit{Intensity}_{2006}$ snapshots. Following Parker and Vogl (2023), 2005 marks the close of the second roll-out phase, so the endpoint is probed only locally; intermediate snapshots are not used as substitutes, because mid-phase the roll-out is still ramping up and such a control would absorb part of the early-phase variation the design isolates. All specifications use the End-of-year numerator over the year-varying municipal household denominator, include Seguro Popular intensity and municipality fixed effects, and are weighted by the population aged 65 and older. The vertical line marks the program's 1997 rollout. The sample is restricted to highly marginalized municipalities. Overlapping year-by-year profiles across the three second-phase-control series, contrasted with the divergent no-control series, indicate that the early-phase coefficient is not sensitive to which later-phase snapshot is conditioned on but is sensitive to omitting a second-phase control altogether. 95\% confidence intervals shown.}
\end{figure}
